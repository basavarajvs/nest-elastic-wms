# Document 6 – Inventory Cycle Count (Part 1)
# Manhattan WMS RF Operations Guide

## 1. Business Overview

### Purpose

Cycle Counting is the process of verifying inventory accuracy while the warehouse remains operational.

Unlike a full physical inventory:

```text
Entire Warehouse
Shutdown Required
```

Cycle Counting allows:

```text
Small Inventory Segments
Checked Daily
```

---

## Why Cycle Counting Exists

Inventory accuracy degrades over time because of:

- Picking mistakes
- Receiving mistakes
- Damage
- Theft
- Misplaced inventory

Cycle Counting identifies discrepancies before they impact customers.

---

## Warehouse Reality

Example:

System believes:

```text
Location A-01-01-01

SKU-A Qty 100
```

Actual inventory:

```text
Qty 95
```

Without cycle counting:

```text
Future picks fail
```

because inventory does not physically exist.

---

## Domain Concepts

### Cycle Count Task

Work generated by Manhattan.

Example:

```text
Count:

Location A-01-01-01
```

---

### Inventory Variance

Difference between:

```text
Expected Quantity
```

and

```text
Actual Quantity
```

---

### Blind Count

Counter does NOT see expected quantity.

Example:

```text
Enter Count
```

instead of:

```text
Expected = 100
```

---

### Why Blind Counting Exists

Prevents operator bias.

---

## High-Level Flow

```text
Generate Count Task
        ↓
Get Work
        ↓
Travel To Location
        ↓
Scan Location
        ↓
Count Inventory
        ↓
Enter Quantity
        ↓
Variance Analysis
        ↓
Approve / Recount
```

---

# Step 1 – Get Count Work

## What User Sees

```text
Inventory Menu

Get Count Work
```

---

## What User Does

Requests next counting assignment.

---

## Why This Step Exists

System determines:

- Which locations require counting
- Count priority
- Count frequency

---

## Domain Concept

Directed Counting

System assigns counts.

Operator executes counts.

---

## Warehouse Reality

Large warehouses may contain:

```text
100,000+ locations
```

Not all locations are counted every day.

---

# Step 2 – Travel To Location

## What User Sees

```text
Count Location

A-01-01-01
```

---

## What User Does

Travels to assigned location.

---

## Why This Step Exists

Inventory must be verified at physical source.

---

## What Breaks If Removed

Inventory cannot be validated.

---

# Step 3 – Scan Location

## What User Sees

```text
Scan Location
```

---

## What User Does

Scans location barcode.

---

## Why This Step Exists

Verifies counter is physically present at correct location.

---

## Manhattan Validation

Verify:

```text
Expected Location
=
Scanned Location
```

---

## Warehouse Reality

Adjacent locations often contain different inventory.

---

# Step 4 – Count Inventory

## What User Sees

Blind Count Example:

```text
Enter Quantity
```

---

## What User Does

Counts inventory physically.

Example:

```text
95 Units
```

---

## Why This Step Exists

System inventory may not equal physical inventory.

---

## Domain Concept

Physical Verification

Inventory records are validated against reality.

---

## Warehouse Example

System:

```text
100 Units
```

Physical:

```text
95 Units
```

Variance:

```text
-5
```

---

# Step 5 – Submit Count

## What User Sees

```text
Confirm Count
```

---

## What User Does

Submits result.

---

## Why This Step Exists

Count becomes official inventory observation.

---

## What Breaks If Removed

No inventory correction can occur.

---

# Step 6 – Variance Detection

## What User Sees

May see:

```text
Variance Found
```

---

## Why This Exists

Inventory discrepancy requires investigation.

---

## Domain Concept

Inventory Accuracy Measurement

Difference between expected and actual inventory.
